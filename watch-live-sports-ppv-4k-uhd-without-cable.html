Hardware Checklist

Before you start, verify that your setup meets the following minimum requirements for a smooth 4K experience.

  1. Internet connection: At least 25 Mbps download speed per 4K stream. Use a wired Ethernet connection whenever possible.
  2. 4K‑capable display: TV or monitor that supports HDMI 2.0 or higher and HDR.
  3. Streaming device: One of the following –
    • Android TV box (8 GB RAM or more)
    • Amazon Fire TV Stick 4K
    • Apple TV 4K
    • Raspberry Pi 4 with 4K output
    • Smart TV with built‑in Android or Linux OS
  4. HDMI 2.0 cable: Certified for 4K 60 Hz.

Step‑by‑Step Setup Guide

1. Prepare Your Network

Log into your router and enable QoS (Quality of Service) for the streaming device’s MAC address. Prioritize video traffic to reduce buffering.

2. Install the Ramix IPTV App

Depending on your device, follow the appropriate method:

  • Android TV / Fire TV:
    1. Open the Google Play Store (or Amazon Appstore).
    2. Search for “Ramix IPTV”.
    3. Select the official app and click Install.
  • Apple TV:
    1. Open the App Store.
    2. Search for “Ramix IPTV”.
    3. Download and install.
  • Raspberry Pi (LibreELEC/Kodi):
    1. Install the latest LibreELEC image.
    2. Boot the Pi and open Kodi.
    3. Navigate to Add‑ons → Install from repository → Video add‑ons → PVR IPTV Simple Client.
    4. Enter the M3U URL provided by Ramix IPTV and save.

3. Activate Your Subscription

Log into the app with the credentials you received after purchase. If you have a trial code, enter it on the “Activate” screen. The app will automatically fetch the latest channel list, including all 4K sports streams.

4. Optimize Video Settings

Inside the app, go to Settings → Video → Output. Choose:

  • Resolution: 3840 × 2160 (4K)
  • Refresh rate: 60 Hz (or match your TV’s native rate)
  • HDR: Auto (if your TV supports it)

Enable “Hardware decoding” to offload processing to the device’s GPU.

5. Test a Live Stream

Select a free 4K channel or a PPV preview. Verify that the picture is sharp, colors are accurate, and there is no audio‑video sync delay. If the stream buffers, revisit the network section.

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Buffering or Stuttering

Typical causes and fixes:

  • Insufficient bandwidth – run a speed test; upgrade your plan if needed.
  • Wi‑Fi interference – switch to 5 GHz band or use Ethernet.
  • Device overload – close background apps, restart the streaming box.

No 4K Option Appears

Make sure your display reports 4K capability to the device. On Android TV, go to Settings → Display → Resolution and confirm 4K is selectable. If not, update the device firmware.

PPV Purchase Not Recognized

After buying a PPV event, the app may need a manual refresh:

  1. Open the app’s “My Purchases” page.
  2. Pull down to refresh or tap the sync icon.
  3. If the event still missing, log out and back in.

Audio Desynchronization

Adjust the audio delay setting in the app (usually under Settings → Audio). A 100‑200 ms offset corrects most sync problems.

Best Practices for a Seamless 4K Sports Experience

  • Schedule regular firmware updates for your streaming device.
  • Use a UPS (uninterruptible power supply) to avoid sudden reboots during live events.
  • Keep the Ramix IPTV app updated to receive the latest server optimizations.
  • Consider a dual‑band router with QoS that can prioritize 4K streams over other household traffic.

FAQ

Do I need a special TV to watch 4K on Ramix IPTV?
No. Any TV that supports HDMI 2.0 and 4K resolution will display the stream. The app will automatically downscale if a lower‑resolution screen is detected.
Can I watch the same PPV event on multiple devices?
Yes, as long as your subscription includes simultaneous streams (most plans allow 2‑4 devices). Each device must be logged in with the same account.
Is there a data cap for 4K streaming?
Ramix IPTV does not impose a data limit, but your ISP may. A 4K hour typically consumes 7‑10 GB, so monitor your monthly usage if you have a capped plan.
What if my internet drops during a live match?
The app uses adaptive bitrate. It will automatically switch to a lower resolution to keep the stream alive, then return to 4K once bandwidth stabilizes.
